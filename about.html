The Three Pillars of Modern Nigerian Elections

📱

1. BVAS Accreditation

The Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) is an electronic handheld device used at all 176,846 Polling Units to verify voter identity using both fingerprint recognition and facial biometric scans.

  • ✓ Eliminates proxy and ghost voting
  • ✓ Operates completely offline during voter accreditation
  • ✓ Total accredited voters are locked before tallying begins
☁️

2. IReV Result Viewing

The INEC Result Viewing Portal (IReV) enables Presiding Officers to capture a digital snapshot of the signed Form EC8A at the Polling Unit and upload it immediately to a publicly accessible repository.

  • ✓ Grants citizens and observers immediate audit visibility
  • ✓ Form EC8A snapshots are timestamped and geo-tagged
  • ✓ Pre-empts tampering during physical transit to collation centers
🏛️

3. Collation Chain

Results are compiled systematically through statutory administrative collation steps governed strictly by the Electoral Act 2022:

  • Level 1: Polling Unit (Form EC8A)
  • Level 2: Ward / Registration Area (Form EC8B)
  • Level 3: LGA Collation Centre (Form EC8C)
  • Level 4: State & National Collation (Form EC8D & EC8E)

Electoral Terminology Guide

Key constitutional and operational concepts clarified for citizen literacy

Under Section 51(2) of the Electoral Act 2022, over-voting occurs when the total number of votes cast at a polling unit exceeds the total number of voters accredited by the BVAS machine at that unit. Where over-voting is established, the result of that specific polling unit must be cancelled.
If the difference in votes between the leading candidate and the runner-up is less than the total number of registered or accredited voters in polling units where voting was cancelled or not held, a winner cannot be returned until supplementary elections are conducted in those affected units.
Under Section 134(2) of the 1999 Constitution (as amended), a presidential candidate must secure: (1) the highest overall number of valid votes cast nationwide, AND (2) at least 25% (one-quarter) of the valid votes cast in each of at least two-thirds of all 36 States and the Federal Capital Territory (at least 24 states + FCT).
Anambra, Bayelsa, Edo, Ekiti, Imo, Kogi, Ondo, and Osun have off-cycle governorship elections because historic election petition tribunals or appellate courts nullified earlier returns and ordered new winners sworn in at later dates. Because a governor's 4-year constitutional term begins on the day they take oath of office, their election cycles became staggered from the general cycle.
